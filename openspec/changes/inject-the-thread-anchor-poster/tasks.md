Tests come from the delta spec, not from the code: per `AGENTS.md`, `openspec-test-writer` is dispatched after this change's spec is approved and **before section 1 begins**. That ordering matters more than usual here, because the behaviour this change adds — refusing establishment rather than anchoring blanks — is the opposite of what the code does today, and every existing test of the establishment path was written against the degrading form. Deriving the new tests from the implementation would reproduce that shape. Section 7 records what the derivation produced and which existing tests had to be corrected because they pin the old policy; only 7.6's corrections follow implementation, because they are corrections rather than derivations.

## 3. One authoritative read for the anchor

- [ ] 3.1 Give `ensure_launch_thread` a keyword-only `read_product: Callable[[], Awaitable[Any]] | None` — nullary, like `hold_lock`, because the adapter binds the session — and call it **after** the lock is held and **after** the early return for a launch that already carries `slack_thread_id`. Placement is the requirement, not an optimisation: `launch-instance`'s last clause says a launch with a thread never resolves its product for the anchor, and it is what bounds this change's whole risk surface.
- [ ] 3.2 Type the product `Any`. `.importlinter` bars `launch` from naming the catalog's domain type, which is why the four call sites already hold theirs as `Any`; the port answers what the composition root's reader answers.
- [ ] 3.3 Compose the anchor from that product: its `name`, `sku.value` and `marketplace_id.value`, plus the launch date `ensure_launch_thread` already reads off the launch. `_compose_anchor_message` keeps its wording and its `Launch Date: TBD` fallback — `design.md` puts the wording out of scope, and `launch-entry`:10 governs it.
- [ ] 3.4 Refuse where the product cannot be resolved: `read_product` absent, raising, or answering `None` are one case. Raise `RuntimeError` naming the product identifier and which of the three occurred, reading consistently with the `no launch found for product …` raise three lines above. No new exception type — `design.md` records why, and no caller catches by type.
- [ ] 3.5 Do **not** apply `getattr` tolerances to the product's fields. The four call sites use them today because they accept a product that may be `None` or a test double that models less than the real one; refusing on an unresolvable product is what makes the tolerance unnecessary, and keeping it would let a double satisfy a check the real store does not — the shape of the defect `fix-launch-thread-mentions`'s task 6.5 corrected.
- [ ] 3.6 Confirm the raise leaves nothing persisted: the anchor is not posted, `launch.slack_thread_id` is not set, `launch_store.save` is not called, and the surrounding `transaction()` in `launch_thread_delivery` rolls back. A refusal that persisted a half-established thread would be worse than the defect.

- [ ] 4.5 Comment that global with the two things a reader needs and cannot infer. ## 6. Composition roots

- [ ] 6.1 `main.py` — supply a **session-taking** reader next to the existing `launch_thread_delivery.read_people` assignment: one that builds `CatalogProductRepository` on the session it is handed, rather than `_RequestScopedCatalog().get_by_id`, which opens its own. `_register_catalog_product` (`:114-141`) is the precedent — the composition root already builds the catalog's store on the launch adapter's own session, for the neighbouring reason.
- [ ] 6.2 `worker.py` — the same, and specifically **not** `_read_catalog_product`, whose docstring states in as many words that *"It opens its own session — the pass may run for many launches, and this read is not part of their transaction"* (`:99-100`). Under this change the read *is* part of a transaction, so reusing it would falsify that sentence and check out a second connection under the lock. Supply a sibling taking the session, keeping `AccessScope.unrestricted()` as `_read_catalog_product` does.
- [ ] 6.3 Both roots, not one, and check the **shape** as well as the presence. The launch confirmation and the gate ask run in the HTTP process; the automation pass's two threaded messages run in the worker. A root that injected only `read_people` would leave every launch started in that process unable to establish a thread at all — which the refusal policy makes a hard failure rather than a degraded one.

  Presence alone is not enough, because this global's arity differs from the five other `read_product` globals and the two obvious readers to reach for — `_RequestScopedCatalog().get_by_id` and `_read_catalog_product` — carry the *old* one and are already imported in the roots that must not use them. An arity mismatch raises `TypeError` inside the closure, which task 3.4 classifies as the "raising" case, so it presents as a catalog fault and retries forever. That is precisely the shape `launch-step-automation`:201 already records: *"A delivery path that requires an identifier in a form the store does not produce delivers nothing at all, while satisfying any test that supplies the form it wants."* So assert that what is wired takes `(session, product_id)` — a signature check, or a test that calls the wired global with both — rather than that the assignment exists.
- [ ] 6.4 Confirm neither reader applies a narrowing access scope, so neither can answer `None` for a product that exists. `main.py`'s reads the repository directly (`:181-183` today); `worker.py`'s passes `AccessScope.unrestricted()`. Both matter: under the refusal policy, a scope that filtered a product would refuse a thread rather than name the product by identifier.
