## Context

See `proposal.md` for the four defects and the evidence for each. Five things about the code as it stands shape this design:

- **The roster→service-identity translation already exists, once, on the ClickUp side.** `clickup_sync._clickup_users` takes a step's `assignees` (roster identifiers), looks each up among the people an injected reader returns, reads `clickup_user_id` off the person, and skips-and-reports anyone the roster does not carry or carries without an account. The Slack side of the same problem — a step's `confirmer` (a roster identifier) and the person's `slack_identity` — was never written. This change writes it, and makes the same *kind* of trade at each of three failure points: the gap is reported, never silently swallowed, and the message goes out regardless. What the message does about the missing tag then differs by path, for a reason stated with each.
- **`launch` may not construct `access`'s store.** `.importlinter` confines it to `access.application`'s public surface, and only a composition root may hold the store. Every existing roster consumer in `launch` therefore takes an injected reader on a module global — `automation_confirmation.read_people`, `gate_confirmation.read_people`, `clickup_sync_job.read_people`, `gate_progression_job.read_people`, `slack_entry.read_people`, `clickup_webhook.read_people` — assigned by `main.py` and `worker.py`. This change adds one more instance of that pattern, not a new mechanism.
- **The reader port and its identifier helper are already in `launch`'s application layer.** `playbook_authoring` defines `RosterReader` as a one-member `Protocol` (`list_people()`), `UnreadableRosterError` for a collaborator of the wrong shape, and the public `person_identifier(person)` that reads a person's generated identifier across the two spellings a reader might use. `automated_decisions.py` already matches `step.confirmer` against `person_identifier(person)` — that is the authoritative statement of which namespace `confirmer` lives in, and this design resolves against exactly the same pair so the mention and the decision check can never disagree about who the confirmer is.
- **`resolve_mention_target` already takes its collaborators as injected ports, and `ensure_launch_thread` beside it takes `hold_lock` and `channel` the same way.** The application layer names ports; `launch_thread_delivery.py` (driven) is the half allowed to bind concrete ones. The roster reader follows that established split rather than being reached for inside the application layer.
- **The pending-result path has two readers of one identifier that disagree about its form.** `deliver_pending_result` rejects anything that is not a `ProductId`; `_decision_value`, on the same object, writes `str(...)`, which is right only for the `uuid.UUID` a stored row actually carries. Whichever form a caller supplies, one of the two is wrong. That is the shape of defect 4, and it is why the fix is to name the form once at the seam rather than to relax either reader.

## Goals / Non-Goals

**Goals:**
- A mention that Slack resolves to the person the step names, on both message kinds whose purpose is to notify a named person.
- A pending result that is actually delivered, from the form the store hands back, with controls that resolve to the result they were composed for.
- A started launch whose submitter is told, whatever happens to the thread.
- A vocabulary value that cannot be rendered wrongly by the ordinary means, rather than one that merely must not be.

**Non-Goals:**
- Not changing `resolve_mention_target`'s rule about *who* to tag where the confirmer resolves: a step's confirmer if it names one, the launch's submitter otherwise. That rule is correct, and only the form its answer is returned in is wrong. What each caller does when the confirmer does **not** resolve is new, and is specified per path.
- Not touching the gate-ask path, which passes `step=None`, already reads `product_id.value`, and is correct as it stands.
- Not changing which messages are threaded, or how the anchor is composed — `inject-the-thread-anchor-poster` owns caller-supplied anchor composition and will retire the two fallbacks corrected here.
- Not introducing a `SlackIdentity` value object (see the decision below).
- Not revisiting `subcategory-advisor`'s own wording, which stays where it is.

## Decisions

**"Resolvable for tagging" is three conditions, not one, and the third is the one that matters.** A named confirmer can be tagged only where the roster carries them, carries them with a `slack_identity`, and carries them **active**. The three failure points are not equally reachable, and saying so is part of the design rather than a caveat on it:

- *Deactivated* is the reachable, durable one. `roster`:72 keeps a deactivated entry's identity data intact, so their `slack_identity` survives; `playbook-authoring`:145-146 specifies that a step whose confirmer is deactivated **keeps being served and its automated results continue to be held pending**. This is a state the specifications guarantee occurs and persists — not an edge case.
- *Not carried at all* is reachable by exactly one sanctioned route. Neither deletion (`roster`:72 offers none) nor a validated write (`launch-playbook`:501 rejects an unknown identifier) produces it, but `playbook-authoring`:107 case 2 permits a write with **no roster supplied**, explicitly meaning "the confirmer precondition is not being evaluated here". A step written that way can name anybody.
- *Carried without a Slack identity* is forbidden by `roster`:10 and enforced in `Person.faults()`. The branch is kept as defence-in-depth and is labelled as such — it costs one condition and it is what a reader will look for — but it is not load-bearing, and the "two failure points" framing an earlier draft rested on was wrong about which states exist.

**The pending-result ask tags nobody when the confirmer is not resolvable, and reports the gap.** The message is composed and posted exactly as it would be otherwise, minus the `<@…>` token, and a warning names the step, the launch and the unresolvable confirmer — `_clickup_users`'s trade, for the same reason it makes it: a failed run would hide a data gap behind a retry, and the run record only says whether the pass succeeded.

Explicitly **not** falling back to the submitter here, though that is the neighbouring rule for a step naming *no* confirmer. `launch-step-automation`:225 accepts a decision "only from the Slack identity belonging to that step's named confirmer, and only where the confirmer is still active on the roster", so tagging anyone else summons a person whose buttons are guaranteed to answer "That decision was refused" — the one outcome worse than not being summoned. And the two situations are different facts that must not read identically in the channel: "this step has no confirmer, so the submitter is being asked" is routine, and "this step names a confirmer who cannot be reached" is a data gap somebody must fix. Confirmed with the user.

The same `:225` clause is what settles the deactivated case, and settles it against an earlier draft of this design, which tagged a deactivated confirmer normally on the reasoning that it kept the authoring problem visible. That was wrong on its own terms: a decision from a deactivated confirmer is refused, so tagging them produces precisely the guaranteed-refused summons the paragraph above rejects the submitter fallback to avoid — and the visibility argument buys nothing, because `playbook-authoring`:146 already guarantees the result stays held pending until an admin reassigns the step. A deactivated confirmer is therefore not resolvable for tagging. Confirmed with the user.

**The stuck-step report falls back to the submitter and names the gap in its text — the opposite disposition, on purpose.** The argument above turns entirely on `:225`, and `:225` governs only decisions on a pending result. Nothing constrains who may act on a stuck step: that report exists *"so that a person can supply what the handler is missing"*, so reaching somebody is the whole of its purpose and an untagged report reaching nobody defeats it. So this path tags the submitter, and carries a line naming that the step's confirmer could not be resolved.

That line is what preserves the second half of the ask's reasoning without paying the first half's cost: a reader can still tell a step naming no confirmer from one whose confirmer cannot be reached, which is the distinction the no-fallback rule existed to protect. Inheriting the ask's policy here — which an earlier draft did — would have applied an authorization argument to a message with no authorization rule, and traded the message's entire purpose for a consistency nobody needs. Confirmed with the user.

**One definition of resolvable, two fallback policies, and no change to the return shape at all.** The two policies need no new type and no new parameter, because each caller already holds everything it needs to tell the cases apart. `resolve_mention_target` keeps answering `str | None`, and "a named confirmer that could not be resolved" is exactly `step.confirmer is set and the answer is None` — a step naming no confirmer yields the submitter instead, so the two can never be confused. `_report_stuck_step` already takes both `step` and `launch`, so it substitutes `launch.submitter` and composes the extra line itself; `deliver_pending_result` already takes `step`, and simply drops the tag.

A richer return — a frozen `(identity, unresolved_confirmer)` result — was drafted and discarded once this became clear. It would have changed the shape every one of the four call sites unpacks, which makes the gate ask and the launch confirmation into files this change edits for no reason of their own, and it would have moved a fact the callers can already derive into a type that exists to carry it. A boolean parameter selecting the policy inside the function was rejected for the opposite reason: it would put the difference in the least visible place. Policy in the two callers, facts in the one function, and the other two call sites untouched.

This is also why the gate ask stays genuinely out of scope rather than out of scope except for a mechanical edit.

**The roster is read only when a step names a confirmer.** The submitter branch needs no translation — `launch.submitter` is recorded from `body["user"]["id"]` and is already a Slack identity — so `resolve_mention_target` returns it without touching the reader. This matters beyond efficiency: the gate ask, the launch confirmation, and every step naming no confirmer keep working through a roster outage or a missing injection, and only the branch that genuinely needs a translation can be degraded by one.

**A roster that cannot be read degrades the mention, never the message.** A reader that is absent (never injected), the wrong shape (`UnreadableRosterError`), or failing is caught and reported, and each path then applies its own fallback — the ask carries no mention, the stuck-step report tags the submitter and names the gap, exactly as when a confirmer resolves to nobody. An unreadable roster is not an authorization fact, so it cannot justify withholding the tag from a message that has no authorization rule; it is simply one more way the confirmer fails to resolve.

This is the opposite disposition from `automation_confirmation._roster_or_fail`, which raises — deliberately, and the two are not in tension: there, the roster read *is* the decision, and an unjudged decision must not be reported as a judgement; here, the roster read is an embellishment on a message whose substance (which product, which step, what the handler produced) does not depend on it. Failing the whole delivery to avoid a degraded mention would trade a message somebody might miss for no message at all.

**No `SlackIdentity` value object.** A nominal type is the structural fix for a namespace confusion, and it was considered. It is declined here on scope: it would have to land on `Person.slack_identity`, on `Launch.submitter`, on the `launch_positions.submitter` column's round-trip, on `decided_by`, and on every `<@…>` site in two modules — a change larger than this one, touching persistence, and independently reviewable. What this change does instead is remove the only place the translation can be skipped: after it, exactly one function converts a roster identifier to a Slack identity, and it is the one whose docstring already claimed to. The value object is recorded here as the follow-up it is, not smuggled in.

**The pending-result seam names the identifier once, on the caller's side.** `_deliver_waiting` already builds `ProductId(str(row.product_id))` for its catalog read, one line above the delivery call, with a comment stating the principle: *"The row carries the identifier as the database spells it; the catalog read wants the value object. A stored row is read back long after the pass that wrote it, so the conversion belongs here rather than being assumed of whatever produced the row."* That principle is right and was simply not applied to the second consumer. So `deliver_pending_result` gains `product_id: ProductId` as a keyword parameter and stops reading it off `result`; the `isinstance` rejection goes with it, having been a guard against the caller its own module never had.

The same parameter feeds `compose_blocks` and `_decision_value`, so the button payload is `product_id.value` — one form, decided at the seam, rather than two readers guessing. This also settles which of the two round-trips is authoritative: the controls carry the value the result was stored against, which is what `_handle_decision`'s `ProductId(str(product_id))` reconstructs and what `accept_automated_result` looks up.

The alternative — teaching `deliver_pending_result` to normalise a `ProductId`, a `UUID` or a `str` — was rejected. It would make every caller's mistake invisible again, which is the property that let this defect ship, and it would leave `_decision_value` still guessing.

**Fixing defect 4 releases a backlog, and the backlog is delivered.** Delivery has never once succeeded, so every pending result ever stored is sitting with `delivered_at` unstamped, being retried on every pass. The first pass in which delivery works flushes all of them at once. That is the correct behaviour and not merely the convenient one: *"An undelivered result is delivered again later"* is the requirement, and a result nobody has ever been asked about is exactly what it is for. Suppressing the backlog would mean marking results delivered that were not, which is the one thing the requirement's own reasoning forbids.

The volume is bounded by how long the defect has existed, not by how long the system has. The `isinstance` guard was introduced by `thread-launch-slack-notifications` itself — `git log -S'must be ProductId, got'` names one commit, `4a0210a fix(thread-launch-slack-notifications): complete product_id threading and fix architecture violations` — which archived 2026-08-31, one day before this change was proposed. So the accumulation is roughly one day of automated passes over the launches in play. That premise is stated rather than assumed because the bound rests entirely on it; task 5.4 measures the result rather than trusting the arithmetic. Small enough not to need throttling, and the task below establishes the actual count before deploy rather than discovering it in the channel. Some of the flushed asks may name steps the served playbook no longer defines; those are already handled — `launch-step-automation`:336 refuses such a decision and voids the row, deliberately, rather than misattributing it.

**A vocabulary value's textual form is its value, enforced by giving it one.** `shared-vocabulary` gains the requirement in two halves. The first — rendering a single-valued vocabulary object yields its value — is implemented as `__str__` on the five identity objects and on `Discipline` and `Severity`, so `f"{product_id}"`, `str(product_id)` and `"%s" % product_id` all produce the value. The second half is the prohibition the proposal lifts out of `subcategory-advisor`: text a person reads or a machine parses is never composed from the object's debugging representation. `__repr__` is untouched and stays the dataclass/enum repr, which is what a debugger, a traceback and a `%r` log line want.

Stating only the prohibition was considered and is not enough on its own: it has been stated once already, in `subcategory-advisor`, and the mistake was made three more times outside that capability's scope. A rule obeyed only by remembering it will be broken again; a rule that makes the careless spelling correct cannot be. Fixing the two `str(product_id)` sites anyway, as the proposal commits to, is not thereby redundant — `.value` says what the site means, and a reader should not have to know about `__str__` to see that the line is right.

**The blast radius of that `__str__` was audited before this design was accepted, not after.** The claim that it needs no migration is only worth making with the sweep behind it, so the sweep was done over `src/`: every `str()` call and every f-string or `%s` interpolation over the seven affected types.

**Corrected during implementation: five sites, not four.** The sweep's first pass used a pattern that matched a bare name but not dotted attribute access, so `f"{launch.product_id}"` and its kind fell through it while `f"{product_id}"` was caught. Re-running with `[a-zA-Z_.]*` found one more — `automation_pass.py:667`, the `BackoffStoreUnrestorable` message, which named the product as `ProductId(value='…')` inside an exception a person reads. The finding does not change the conclusion, and is recorded rather than quietly amended because a count stated as exhaustive has to be corrected in public when it turns out not to have been.

Four of the five are messages or error text that strictly improve — `launch_playbook.py:391`'s `f"discipline '{self.discipline}'"` stops saying `Discipline.LISTING` and starts saying `listing`, `automation_pass.py:667` as above, and the two `str(product_id)` sites this change already corrects. One wants the debugging form and must say so: `use_cases.py:342` composes `f"product '{product_id.value}' ({product_id})"`, deliberately showing the raw value and the typed object side by side, and becomes a redundant `'abc' (abc)` unless it switches to `!r`.

Separately, sixteen logging call sites pass one of these objects as a `%s` argument. Every one is a diagnostic that reads better for the change and none is a record anything parses, so they are counted but not touched.

Templates were swept too, since a `{{ product_id }}` is neither a `str()` call nor an f-string and three capabilities render admin pages. Eleven template sites interpolate one of the seven types, and every one of them receives a value its view model has already unwrapped: `playbook_admin._row` passes `definition.discipline.value`, `_row_identity` passes `sku.value`, and `product_dossier.py:300-317` wraps each of sku, marketplace and asin in `str(getattr(…, "value", …))` before the template ever sees it. So the templates change not at all, and the sweep's four sites are four rather than fifteen.

No persisted record and no parsed payload is composed by rendering one of these objects: the only control payload, `_decision_value`, is fed a `uuid.UUID` today (not a vocabulary type, so unaffected) and is explicitly moved to `product_id.value` by this change. `launch_admin.py`'s `str(sku)` and `f"{PAGE_PATH}/{product_id}"` operate on values already unwrapped before they arrive. So there is no *persisted or parsed* old-form/new-form split for any reader to reconcile, and "no migration" is a finding rather than an assumption.

The qualification is deliberate, because one old-form artifact can already exist and this change does not reach it: a thread anchor posted before this lands, for a launch whose first message was a stuck-step report at a moment the catalog read returned nothing, carries `ProductId(value='…')` as its permanent heading — that is defect 2, and `launch-instance`:513 forbids re-creating the anchor to correct it. Bounded and small (it needs that exact sequence), corrected for every future thread, and not a migration: nothing reads those headings but a person.

**Values with no single value are out of the first half.** `Launching(phase=2)` and `SteadyState(posture=Hold)` carry more than one value; `Development` and `Retired` carry none; an access scope is either a distinct unrestricted construction or a set. None of them has a single value to render as, so there is nothing for `__str__` to return that is not an invented format — and inventing one would be this requirement choosing a format rather than stating one. The requirement is scoped to the seven single-valued objects; the prohibition half still binds all the rest, which must be named by their parts.

**The launch confirmation's last resort is the direct message the failure path already uses.** Where thread establishment or the threaded reply fails, `slack_entry` posts `_confirmation_text(submission)` to the submitter by the same `_post(client, submitter, …)` call `_failure_text` uses, and the thread failure is logged as it is today. The text is not altered to mention the missing thread: the submitter needs to know the launch started, and a line about Slack plumbing they can do nothing about is noise on the one message that has to land. Where the direct message fails too, the failure is logged and nothing further is attempted — there is no third channel, and the launch stands regardless, which is what the surrounding `try` already guarantees.

This restores the symmetry the proposal names: a failed start reaches the submitter directly and always, and now so does a successful one.

The fallback fires on any exception around the thread reply, including one raised after Slack accepted the post — a timeout on a request that landed — so a submitter will occasionally be told twice. Accepted, and the same trade `launch-step-automation`'s stuck-step requirement already makes for two overlapping passes: a duplicate message is the cost of never losing the one that matters, and the alternative (confirming the post before reporting failure) buys a rarer duplicate for a real chance of silence.

**The roster reader reaches `launch_thread_delivery` as a module global from both roots.** `main.py` assigns `_RosterReader()`; `worker.py` reuses `clickup_sync_job.read_people`, as it already does for `gate_progression_job`. Both are needed: the launch confirmation and the gate ask run in the HTTP process, the automation pass and its two affected messages in the worker. This is one more assignment in the pattern `unify-launch-adapter-dependencies` proposes to retire, and is deliberately consistent with its neighbours rather than anticipating it — a lone collaborator wired differently would be harder to sweep up than one more of the same.

## Risks / Trade-offs

- **`__str__` changes what existing log lines and f-strings print.** Every site that interpolates one of these objects now shows the bare value instead of `ProductId(value='…')`. The audit above bounds this to four sites and finds no persisted or parsed form affected, which is what downgrades it from a risk to a diff; the full suite remains the check, since any test asserting the old rendering fails loudly rather than silently.
- **A test asserting the old rendering may be asserting the defect.** Where one does, it is corrected rather than accommodated — but each such correction must be read, not batched, since a test that expected `ProductId(value='…')` in a *message* was pinning defect 2 and a test expecting it in a `repr` was not.
- **Fixing defect 4 makes defect 1 reachable for the first time.** The pending-result ask has never posted, so its mention has never been rendered in production, and the two fixes must land together or the first message this system ever sends for a pending result will be the mis-tagged one.
- **The two message kinds now degrade differently on the same failure.** An unresolvable confirmer means no tag on the ask and a submitter tag plus a named gap on the stuck-step report. That asymmetry is deliberate and reasoned above, but it is the kind of thing a later reader smooths out for consistency, so both requirements state their own reason rather than cross-referencing one another.
- **The mention path acquires a roster read on the automation pass's hot path.** One `list_people()` per message that names a confirmer, not per step — the pass already reads the roster once per ClickUp projection, and the messages this affects are rare by construction (a step needing confirmation, or a step that has stopped moving). No caching is introduced; a reader that turns out to be expensive is a separate problem with a separate fix.
- **On the ask path, the gap reaches the log and nothing else.** Consistent with `_clickup_users`, and the same limitation: nobody watching the channel learns that the ask's confirmer could not be resolved, and the result stays held pending until someone reads the log or notices the thread. The stuck-step report does not share this — it names the gap in its own text — but making the ask do likewise would put a configuration fault into a message asking for a product decision. Surfacing such gaps in Slack properly is `clickup_sync_job`'s existing Custom-Field-gap report pattern, and would be a change of its own.

## Migration Plan

No migration, and the claim is audited rather than assumed. No schema change, no new runtime variable, no stored form altered — `step.confirmer` keeps holding the roster identifier it holds today, and this change is about how it is read. The `__str__` sweep (above) confirms no persisted record or parsed payload changes shape, which is the only route by which this change could have needed one. Both new wiring lines are assignments in composition roots that already construct the collaborator they assign.

The one thing that *does* change at deploy is a one-time release of undelivered pending results, reasoned above and bounded by roughly a day. Establish the count before deploying rather than after.

Rollout order within the change: the `shared-vocabulary` textual form first (inert on its own, and it is what makes the two `str(product_id)` corrections verifiable), then the pending-result seam, then the roster-backed mention, then the launch-entry last resort. Each is independently green, so a failure at any point leaves a coherent tree.
